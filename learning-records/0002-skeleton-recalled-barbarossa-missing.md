# The two-wars skeleton is held, except for Barbarossa and the USSR switching sides

The user can tell the war in four dated sentences from memory: two separate starts (1937 Asia, 1939 Europe), the merge in 1941 through Pearl Harbor and Germany's declaration of war on the US, and the end in 1945. This confirms the lesson 1 framework is in place.

**Evidence**: Free-recall answer after lesson 1: "In 1937, Japan and China began their full-scale war against each other. In 1939, Germany and the USSR invaded Poland. In 1941, Japan attacked the USA and Germany declared war on the USA, merging the two wars into the world war. In 1945, both Japan and Germany surrendered."

**Gaps**:
- Barbarossa (June 1941) was left out. As written, the story leaves the USSR on Germany's side, which is the most important gap.
- Britain and France's declarations of war were left out, though they are what turned the invasion of Poland into a war.
- The two endings were merged into one ("both surrendered"), losing Germany in May and Japan in August and September.

**Implications**: Lesson 2 (causes) can go ahead. Its quiz should return to Barbarossa and the USSR changing sides, and to the order of the surrenders, as spaced retrieval.
