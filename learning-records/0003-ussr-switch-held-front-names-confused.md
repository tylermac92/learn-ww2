# The USSR's switch is now held; two terms need correcting ("Axis" and "Eastern Front")

The user now places the USSR correctly: on Germany's side in 1939, on the Allied side in 1945, switched by Germany's attack in 1941 (they described Barbarossa but didn't name it). From the Scanlon reading they gave a good reason for Hitler's declaration of war: Japan would keep the US busy, so it was a good moment for a showdown Germany saw as inevitable.

**Misconceptions corrected**:
- They called the 1939 USSR "Axis". It was Germany's partner under the German-Soviet Pact, but it never joined the Axis: the talks in Berlin in November 1940 ended with no agreement.
- They used "Eastern front" for the war against Japan and "Western front" for the war against Germany, seen from the American side. In WWII usage, the **Eastern Front** means Germany against the USSR. The US fought in the **Pacific** and **European** theatres.
- They said Germany declared war "because of" Pearl Harbor, as if it had to. The Tripartite Pact didn't oblige Germany to join a war Japan started. It was a choice.

**Implications**: Start the glossary with these terms (Axis, Allies, Eastern Front, theatre, Operation Barbarossa) and use them strictly from lesson 2 on. Check "Eastern Front" again in a later quiz.
