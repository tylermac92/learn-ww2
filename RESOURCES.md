# World War II Resources

## Knowledge

### Reference (free, high-trust, for checking facts)
- [The National WWII Museum: articles](https://www.nationalww2museum.org/war/articles)
  Short essays by museum historians, with citations. Use for: single events and decisions. Example used in lesson 1: [Sean Scanlon, "Hitler's Declaration of War on the United States" (2024)](https://www.nationalww2museum.org/war/articles/hitlers-declaration-war-united-states).
- [The National WWII Museum: V-J Day fact sheet (PDF)](https://www.nationalww2museum.org/sites/default/files/2017-07/vj-day-fact-sheet.pdf)
  Use for: how and when the war ended in 1945.
- [USHMM Holocaust Encyclopedia](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/)
  The US Holocaust Memorial Museum's encyclopedia. Use for: Nazi Germany, the German-Soviet Pact, the invasion of the USSR, the Holocaust. Example: [Invasion of the Soviet Union, June 1941](https://encyclopedia.ushmm.org/content/en/article/invasion-of-the-soviet-union-june-1941).
- [Encyclopaedia Britannica: World War II](https://www.britannica.com/event/World-War-II)
  A long survey article in many sections. Use for: overall framing, dates, the sides. Related: [Marco Polo Bridge Incident](https://www.britannica.com/event/Marco-Polo-Bridge-Incident), [Mukden Incident](https://www.britannica.com/event/Mukden-Incident).
- [Imperial War Museums: history articles](https://www.iwm.org.uk/history)
  The UK national war museum. Use for: a British view, and the Home Front.

### Books (one-volume histories of the whole war)
- _The Second World War_ by Antony Beevor (2012)
  A widely praised one-volume narrative that covers every front; it can be read straight through or dipped into. **Default recommendation once the skeleton is solid.**
- _The Storm of War_ by Andrew Roberts (2009)
  About 600 pages, praised for a clear narrative of how and why the war happened. Use for: a faster read than Beevor.
- _The Second World War_ by John Keegan (1989)
  Readable, with a focus on the human side. Use for: a shorter overview.
- _Inferno: The World at War, 1939–1945_ by Max Hastings (2011)
  Built around eyewitness accounts. Use for: what the war was like for the people who lived it.
- _A World at Arms_ by Gerhard Weinberg (1994)
  Over 1,100 pages; the scholars' one-volume history. Use for: later, once the user wants depth and global strategy.

### Documentary
- [_The World at War_ (Thames Television, 1973–74)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_World_at_War)
  26 episodes of about 52 minutes, narrated by Laurence Olivier and made with the Imperial War Museum. Built on interviews with people who were there. Use for: the main documentary companion to the lessons, episode by episode.
